1. Data you definitely need
A. Parcel boundaries

You need GIS polygons showing each legal property parcel.

Collect:

parcel ID
legal land description
parcel geometry
area
municipality
land classification

Why: This is the map layer that connects ownership records to crop, ecological, census, LiDAR and satellite data.

B. Current land ownership

Collect:

registered owner name
owner type: individual, company, government, trust, Indigenous organization
ownership share
title number
parcel ID
acquisition date, when available

Why: The project wants to identify who currently controls farmland and whether ownership is concentrated among fewer owners.

C. Historical ownership and transfers

This is probably the most important purchased dataset.

Collect:

previous owner
new owner
transfer/registration date
sale price, where available
transfer or instrument number
title creation/cancellation dates
mortgage or lender information, where relevant

Why: You cannot study financialization or consolidation using current ownership alone. You need to see how ownership changed over time.

D. Corporate ownership information

When a title is owned by a company, collect:

legal company name
corporation number
directors or registered office, where available
parent company
subsidiaries
incorporation jurisdiction

Why: One investor may use several numbered companies, making ownership look less concentrated than it actually is.

2. Agricultural data you should collect

These may be free or lower cost:

crop type by year
agricultural land classification
pasture and grazing land
farm size
land tenure: owned versus rented
crop production
irrigation
assessed farmland value
farmland sale value
agricultural Crown land
soil capability

Why: The job ad specifically mentions categorical data such as land titles and crop production.

3. Ecological data

To study “correlations between land transfers and ecological changes,” collect:

wetlands
grasslands
tree and shelterbelt cover
surface water
land-cover change
field-boundary change
soil
slope and elevation
drainage
vegetation condition

Satellite imagery can help measure these changes from around 2000 onward.

4. Social data

To examine social changes, collect data at the municipality, census division or community level:

number of farms
average farm size
farmland prices
farmer age
population change
farm employment
young/new farmers
rented versus owned farmland
proximity to reserves, Treaty lands and Indigenous territories
